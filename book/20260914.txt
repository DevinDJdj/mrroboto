@@Why did they want to revolt?

==
Based on the provided text, the motivation to revolt was complex and multi-layered, combining deep resentment over loss of autonomy with opportunistic appeals based on local interests.

Here are the primary reasons for the Ionian people's desire to revolt:

 ## 1. Resistance to Persian Rule (The Core Motivation)
The fundamental cause was the desire for independence from *King Darius* and the ruling authority established by the Persians. The text repeatedly emphasizes that the revolution was a rejection of the central power structure ("all Ionia has revolted from the king").

 ## 2. Political Instability and Opportunity
The period was marked by intense political maneuvering involving exiles, which heightened local tensions:

 *   *External Manipulation** Exiled figures like Hippias stirred up "enmity between the Athenians and Artaphrenes," creating a climate of suspicion and mistrust that the Ionians could capitalize on.
 *   *Lack of Self-Determination** The Ionian sentiment was geared toward achieving freedom from foreign political control, allowing them to chart their own course ("...if ye shall be willing to do as he says; for now all Ionia has revolted from the king...").

 ## 3. Economic and Rhetorical Appeals
The revolt was heavily fueled by propaganda—most notably that delivered by Aristagoras of Miletus. This rhetoric focused on:

 *   *Wealth** Highlighting the immense "wealth which there was in Asia."
 *   *Military Opportunity** Portraying the Persian military system as easily defeated ("how they used neither shield nor spear and were easy to overcome").

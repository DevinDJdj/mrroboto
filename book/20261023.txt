  Description of the Lathe

The lathe described is part of an integrated automated system designed to convert a coil of brass wire into finished pins efficiently.

 **1. The Mechanism:**
    It features a **chuck** which opens to receive and hold the separated piece of wire (the blank pin).
 *   When activated, the machine performs two key mechanical actions: it **revolves rapidly**, and it guides an advancing tool—a **steel mill**—against the rotating wire.

 **2. Its Function in Pin Making:**

The lathe serves several specialized functions during the manufacturing sequence:

    Coarse Pointing (First Stage):** After a piece of wire is cut to the correct length, the forceps moves it into the chuck of the small lathe. While waiting for the next piece of wire, the lathe begins operating, revolving the pin while the steel mill grinds down the projecting end of the wire, performing the coarse pointing.
 *   Fine Pointing (Second Stage):** The process is sequential. Once the first machine stops (after the coarse pointing), the pin is transferred to an **adjacent lathe**. This second machine continues the process by using a finer steel mill to complete the pointing on the blank pin.

In summary, the lathe acts as a specialized rotary tool that mechanically shapes the raw wire blanks through rapid rotation and grinding action, drastically speeding up the labor-intensive processes of pointing the pin.

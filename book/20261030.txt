@@What is Assault?

==
The word **assault** has several meanings depending on the context—most commonly in law, but also generally referring to a forceful attack.

Here are the main definitions:

  1. Legal Context (Most Common Use)
In law, an assault is typically defined as an attempt to physically harm or injure another person without physical contact. It often involves making someone reasonably fear that they are about to be hit or attacked.

 *   Assault *per se***: The act of putting someone in reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact (e.g., waving a weapon menacingly).
 *   (Contrast with Battery):** A related crime, **battery**, requires actual physical contact with the victim. Therefore, one can commit assault without touching the person, but committing battery *requires* touch.

 ### 2. General/Literal Context
In everyday language, an assault refers to:

    A sudden, forceful attack or onslaught directed against a place, object, system, or idea.
    *   Example:* "The rebellion was met with a military **assault**." (Here, 'assault' means aggressive action.)
 *   A major attempt or effort; a push.

 ### 3. Figurative Context
Figuratively, it can mean an attack on ideas or reputation:

    Example:* "The book was under constant **assault** from critics." (Meaning: subject to criticism/attack.)

@@We're steam carriages common and is this just another way to say what we would think of as a car?

==
Yes, you are correct in your modern interpretation. In simple terms, a **steam carriage** as discussed in this 19th-century text was an early form of what we today call an **automobile** or **car**.

Here is a more detailed explanation of the historical context:

  The Terminology Shift

 *   Steam Carriage:** This term was used because the power source for propulsion was *steam*, generated by burning fuel (like coal) to heat water in a boiler, which then drove pistons and wheels. It literally described the mechanism powering the vehicle.
 *   Automobile/Car:** These terms emerged later as internal combustion engines became dominant. The invention of the gasoline-powered engine allowed vehicles to operate without large boilers and complex mechanical systems visible to the casual observer, leading to a naming convention based on the function (a self-propelled wheeled *auto*) rather than the fuel source (steam).

 ### Context in the Passage

The passage you provided illustrates this perfectly. The committee studying the steam carriages wasn't just asking about how they looked; they were conducting a rigorous **engineering and economic assessment**:

1.  **Tolls:** They had to calculate exactly *how much* these new machines damaged public infrastructure (the roads).
2.  **Wear and Tear Analysis:** Mr. Macneill’s meticulous calculations distinguishing between wear caused by horses' feet versus wheels, and comparing fast coaches vs. heavy wagons, show that the steam carriage was considered a revolutionary technology requiring unprecedented study.

In short, it is entirely accurate to view the "steam carriage" as the direct technological precursor—the ancestor—of the modern car.

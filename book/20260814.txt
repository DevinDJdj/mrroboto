Okay, let's break down the tribal situation surrounding Athens as described in these passages from Xenophon’s *Anabasis*. It’s a surprisingly complex and somewhat chaotic picture!

 *1. Athenian Tribes (Established by Cleisthenes)**

    Archelaoi** Roughly 57 men - This was Cleisthenes' own tribe, reflecting his rule and influence.
 *   *Hyatai** Approximately 60 men – Named after a pig.
 *   *Oneatai** Around 59 men - Named after an ass.
 *   *Choireatai** Roughly 60 men – This one remains somewhat ambiguous but likely related to livestock (Choiros meaning ‘flock’ or ‘herd’).

These tribes formed the core of Athenian political organization and were a significant source of local identities, often leading to rivalry and conflict. They existed for over 60 years after Cleisthenes' reforms.

 *2. Tribes Established by the Sikyonians**

    Hylleis** (Likely referring to Hyllus, an earlier tyrant) – This tribe represented a shift away from traditional Greek identities, reflecting the Sikyonian influence.
 *   *Pamphyloi** (Referring to Pamphylos - a region in Asia Minor). Again, this highlights the Sikyonians’ attempt to distance themselves and their followers from established Greek affiliations.
 *   *Dymanatai** (Likely derived from “Dynamos” – meaning ‘power’) – This tribe is likely based on a common identity among the people of Sicyon.
 *    *Aigialeis** Named after Aigialus, son of Adrastos—linked to a heroic lineage.

 *3. Tribes Around Athens (Beyond the immediate Athenian tribes)**

The text offers glimpses into the larger regional landscape, particularly as it relates to conflict and alliances:

 *   *Chalkidians** These were located along the northern coast near Attica, making them frequent adversaries of Athens. They had their own distinct tribal structure.
 *   *Boeotians**  Located in Boeotia (to the west of Attica), they allied with the Chalkidians and engaged in battles alongside them against Athens. Their tribes are not specifically named in these passages but they are a significant political and military force.
 *   *Corinthians** Situated to the south-west, they played a crucial role in shifting allegiances, famously deserting Cleomenes' army. 
 *   *Lacedaemonians (Spartans)** While not a tribe *within* Attica, their involvement was central to most of these conflicts.

 *Approximate Tribe Sizes (Estimated based on the text’s references)**

    The "seven hundred" of the Boeotian tribe who were captured – a significant number for the time, illustrating the scale of Athenian military action.
 *  “Several” Athenians slain in battles and held as prisoners (during Cleomenes’ invasion). 


 *Important Note** The tribes are frequently used as labels for groups of people rather than strictly defined ethnic or geographic divisions. They were more about social and political affiliation than fixed ancestry.

@@What does it mean to walk about the house in my long robe?

==
Based on the literary context and the overall themes of humility, authenticity, and anti-ostentation found throughout Marcus Aurelius’s writings, the phrase *"to walk about the house in my long robe"* is highly symbolic.

It does not refer to a literal necessity or action; rather, it represents an act associated with *intellectual pretension and affected behavior.*

Here is a detailed breakdown of its likely meaning:

 ## 1. The Meaning of the Robe (Symbolism)
In the context of Roman intellectual life, wearing specific robes could signify status, discipline, or scholarly dedication. However, when the author lists it as something he *abandoned*, the robe becomes less about clothing and more about a *costume for performance.*

  2. The Action of "Walking About" (The Pretense)
Combining the physical act with the garment implies:

 *   *Posing** Strolling around merely to look learned or pensive. It is the outward display of intellectual activity rather than actual, focused study or practical wisdom.
 *   *Affected Behavior** This type of behavior was associated with sophists or dilettantes—people who talked the talk (rhetoric, poetry) but lacked sincere insight or rigorous discipline in their daily lives.

 ## 3. Contextual Conclusion (The Rejection)
Because this passage is part of Marcus Aurelius detailing what he learned from Rusticus, the goal is self-improvement and genuine virtue. By stating that he *did not* use to do this, the author is emphasizing his rejection of:

 *Summary** It means abandoning any habit—whether intellectual or physical—that was merely for *show's sake.* He preferred sincere action (writing letters without affectation) over performance (strolling in robes and discussing profound ideas).

@@What is the nature of man according to this passage?

==
Based on these passages, the nature of man is depicted as a complex union of *reasoning capacity*, *social/charitable inclination*, and an inherent moral potential that strives for self-mastery, justice, and alignment with the "common good."

The text presents several layered definitions:

 ## 1. The Cognitive and Moral Nature (Reason and Virtue)

At its core, man's nature is defined by the *mind’s capacity* to achieve tranquility through proper judgment and discretion. Man is understood to be inherently capable of:

 *   *Right Judgment** Assessing events based on one's actual, true character ("this thou art in very deed"), rather than how he appears to others.
 *   *Reasoning Faculty** Using reason not only for intellectual pursuits but also guiding the *common good*. The mind must reject anything false or uncertain and focus exclusively on what benefits all (Chapter VI).
 *   *Sociable/Charitable Inclination** Man’s actions are properly guided by a faculty dedicated to both self-reasoning and the benefit of others.

 ## 2. The Perfected State (A Life of Virtue)

The highest expression of human nature is found not in external achievements (like fame or titles), but in moral conduct that reflects internal stability, epitomized by:

 *   *Temperance and Moderation** Living without being excessively emotional ("never hot and vehement") nor emotionally detached ("nor yet so cold and stupid").
 *   *Authenticity** Being "free from all manner of dissimulation."
 *   *Focus on Virtue** The essential goal is to become a person governed by principles that make one *just, temperate, courageous, and liberal*.

 ## 3. The Essential Definition (The Common Good)

In the most explicit definition (Chapter VI), man's nature is contrasted with lesser forms of life (like leaves or servile animals). It possesses qualities that are unique and supreme:

 *   *Irresistible Capacity** Man’s nature is part of a "common nature which cannot be hindered."
 *   *Reasonable and Just** He is both reasonable (guided by intellect) and just (bound toward moral uprightness).
 *   *Self-Correcting** Every action must pass the test: *"How will this when it is done agree with me?"* The aim is to live according to the "law of right and reason, by which God Himself is."

 **

  Summary Statement:

In essence, the nature of man is defined as a rational soul that seeks harmony between its internal judgment and its moral duty. It is an entity whose highest calling is to transcend superficial desires (like fame or wealth) and to consistently direct its will toward the *common good*, thereby achieving a state of internal *tranquillity* rooted in virtuous action and moral principle.

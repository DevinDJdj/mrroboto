@@Why is a larger average price related to stock being held by a few people?

==
Based on passages 191 and 196, a larger average price is related to stock being held by few people because **scarcity gives those holders significant control over supply**, allowing them to dictate higher prices through speculation.

Here is a detailed breakdown of the underlying principle:

  1. The Principle of Concentration (Passage 191)
The passage explains that when the entire stock is in the possession of one or very few people, they are not bound by the checks and balances of competition:

    Maximum Profit Goal:** An individual holding the supply will naturally try to set a price that generates the maximum amount of money.
 *   Managing Demand:** While the holder knows that raising the price too high will cause consumers to decrease their consumption, they can sustain an elevated price because buyers have no immediate alternatives.

 ### 2. The Effect of Competition (Passage 191 & 196)
In contrast, when supply is distributed among several dealers:

    Immediate Competition:** There is instant competition between the sellers. This rivalry acts as a check on prices, preventing any single party from raising the price excessively high.
 *   Stabilization:** The passage suggests that only when the whole supply is in many hands can a steady, "uniform average" be struck between the desires of buyers and sellers.

 ### 3. Real-World Example: Speculation (Passage 194 & 196)
The texts use commodities like cajeput oil to illustrate this principle. When few people hold the stock during times of perceived crisis (like a disease outbreak), they can engage in speculation, driving prices dramatically upward—reaching prices far higher than what would be possible if the commodity were widely available and sold by competing merchants.

 **In summary:** Concentration allows for **monopolistic power**, which enables sellers to price goods based on artificial scarcity and perceived necessity, leading to significantly inflated average prices.
